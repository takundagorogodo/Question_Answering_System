COSINE SIMILARITY

1. Cosine similarity measures the similarity between two nonzero vectors.
2. It calculates the cosine of the angle formed by two vectors.
3. Cosine similarity is widely used in natural language processing.
4. It is commonly applied to compare text embeddings.
5. Text embeddings represent words, sentences, or documents as numerical vectors.
6. Similar meanings can produce vectors pointing in similar directions.
7. Cosine similarity focuses on vector direction rather than vector magnitude.
8. The measure is useful for semantic search systems.
9. It can compare a user query with stored document chunks.
10. Search systems often rank results using cosine similarity.
11. A high score suggests that two vectors point in similar directions.
12. A low score suggests weaker directional similarity.
13. Cosine similarity does not prove that two texts have identical meanings.
14. Similarity scores depend on the embedding model.
15. Different embedding models can produce different similarity rankings.
16. Cosine similarity is a mathematical measure, not a probability.
17. It can be used with dense vector representations.
18. It is often used with transformer-based language models.
19. It supports retrieval-augmented generation systems.
20. It can help identify semantically related knowledge-base passages.

SECTION 2: MATHEMATICAL FORMULA
21. Cosine similarity is calculated using the dot product of two vectors.
22. The dot product measures the combined products of corresponding vector components.
23. The magnitude of a vector is its Euclidean length.
24. Cosine similarity divides the dot product by both vector magnitudes.
25. The formula is cosine similarity equals A dot B divided by the product of their magnitudes.
26. The dot product of A and B is the sum of corresponding component products.
27. The magnitude of A is the square root of the sum of squared components.
28. The magnitude of B is calculated in the same way.
29. Both vectors must have the same number of dimensions.
30. Cosine similarity is undefined when either vector has zero magnitude.
31. A zero vector has no direction.
32. Vector normalization can simplify cosine calculations.
33. L2 normalization divides a vector by its Euclidean magnitude.
34. For normalized vectors, cosine similarity equals their dot product.
35. Floating-point calculations may introduce small numerical differences.
36. Implementations should handle zero vectors safely.
37. Similarity calculations can be performed using numerical libraries.
38. Matrix multiplication can calculate many similarities efficiently.
39. Cosine similarity is commonly used with floating-point embeddings.
40. The formula remains the same regardless of what the vectors represent.

SECTION 3: SCORE INTERPRETATION
41. General cosine similarity ranges from negative one to positive one.
42. A score of positive one indicates vectors pointing in the same direction.
43. A score of zero indicates orthogonal vectors.
44. A score of negative one indicates vectors pointing in opposite directions.
45. Many text embedding systems produce nonnegative or mostly positive similarities.

48. Similarity scores should not automatically be interpreted as probabilities.
49. A higher score often indicates a closer semantic match within one retrieval system.

SECTION 14: COMMON QUESTIONS
261. What is cosine similarity? It measures the cosine of the angle between two nonzero vectors.
262. Why is cosine similarity used in NLP? It compares text embeddings for semantic retrieval.
263. Does cosine similarity measure probability? No, it is a similarity score rather than a probability.
264. What does a score of one mean? The vectors point in the same direction.
265. What does a score of zero mean? The vectors are orthogonal.
266. What does a negative score mean? The vectors point in generally opposing directions.
267. Can cosine similarity compare sentences? Yes, when sentences are represented as compatible vectors.
268. Can cosine similarity find paraphrases? It can, if the embedding model represents their meanings similarly.
269. Does a high score guarantee relevance? No, the passage may be related without answering the question.
270. Does a high score guarantee truth? No, factual support must be checked separately.
271. What is an embedding? It is a numerical representation of text or another object.
272. What is semantic search? It retrieves content based on represented meaning.
273. What is top-k retrieval? It returns the k highest-ranked candidates.
274. What is a vector index? It supports efficient searching over stored vectors.
275. What is normalization? It scales a vector to a chosen magnitude, often one.
276. Why handle zero vectors? Cosine similarity is undefined when a vector has zero magnitude.
277. What is hybrid retrieval? It combines lexical and semantic retrieval signals.
278. What is reranking? It reorders retrieved candidates using another relevance measure.
279. What is recall at k? It measures whether relevant results occur in the top k.
280. What is precision at k? It measures the fraction of top-k results that are relevant.
