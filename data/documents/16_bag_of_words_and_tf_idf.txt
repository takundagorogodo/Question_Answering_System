BAG OF WORDS AND TF-IDF

BAG OF WORDS
Bag of Words is commonly abbreviated as BoW.
BoW is a text representation method.
BoW converts text into numerical feature vectors.
BoW creates a vocabulary from a collection of documents.
Each vocabulary term can become one vector dimension.
A document is represented using counts or binary indicators.
Count-based BoW records how many times a word occurs.
Binary BoW records whether a word occurs.
BoW usually ignores word order.
BoW therefore loses some syntactic information.
BoW is simple to implement.
BoW is easy to interpret.
BoW is often used as a baseline text representation.

BOW EXAMPLE
If the vocabulary is ['nlp', 'language', 'model'], each document has three features.
A document containing 'nlp language language' has counts 1, 2, and 0.
The vector length equals the vocabulary size.
Words absent from a document receive zero in a count vector.
Repeated words increase their count.
Vocabulary construction should be consistent between training and testing.
Unknown words may be ignored or mapped to a special token.
Stop-word removal may reduce the vocabulary.
Lowercasing can merge words such as 'NLP' and 'nlp' if appropriate.
Stemming or lemmatization can also reduce vocabulary size.

BOW LIMITATIONS
BoW ignores the order of words.
'Dog bites man' and 'man bites dog' can receive identical word counts.
BoW does not directly encode word meaning.
Synonyms can be represented as separate features.
Polysemous words are not disambiguated by basic BoW.
Large vocabularies create high-dimensional vectors.
BoW vectors are usually sparse.
Sparse vectors contain many zero values.
Large sparse matrices can require efficient storage.
BoW can still perform well on many classification and retrieval tasks.

TF-IDF
TF-IDF stands for Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency.
TF-IDF assigns a weight to a term in a document.
Term Frequency measures how often a term occurs in a document.
Inverse Document Frequency measures how rare a term is across documents.
TF-IDF is commonly calculated as TF multiplied by IDF.
A term frequent in one document can receive a high TF.
A term appearing in many documents receives a lower IDF.
A term appearing in few documents receives a higher IDF.
Common words often receive lower TF-IDF weights.
Domain-specific terms can receive higher weights when they are uncommon.

TF FORMULAS AND INTUITION
A simple TF formula divides term count by document length.
IDF is commonly based on the total number of documents.
Document frequency is the number of documents containing the term.
The logarithm in IDF reduces the scale of frequency differences.
Different software libraries can use slightly different TF-IDF formulas.
Some implementations smooth the IDF calculation.
TF-IDF is not a probability.
TF-IDF is a feature weight.
TF-IDF values depend on the document collection.
Adding documents can change IDF values.

SIMILARITY AND RETRIEVAL
Cosine similarity is commonly used with TF-IDF vectors.
Cosine similarity compares the orientation of two vectors.
A higher cosine similarity generally indicates greater vector similarity.
TF-IDF can rank documents for a search query.
A query can be converted into the same vector space as documents.
Documents sharing important terms with a query can receive higher scores.
TF-IDF retrieval is lexical rather than deeply semantic.
Synonyms may not match if their words are different.
TF-IDF can be combined with other retrieval signals.
TF-IDF is useful for small and medium educational knowledge bases.

QUESTION ANSWERING
BoW can provide a simple baseline for question retrieval.
TF-IDF can identify documents containing important question terms.
A question can be compared with stored NLP explanations.
High-weight technical terms can help retrieve relevant content.
Candidate paragraphs can be ranked using cosine similarity.
NER can be combined with TF-IDF to improve query understanding.
N-grams can be combined with TF-IDF to preserve some phrase information.
Word embeddings can complement TF-IDF by adding semantic similarity.
A hybrid retriever can combine lexical and semantic scores.
TF-IDF does not generate an answer by itself.
