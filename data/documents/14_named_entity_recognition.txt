NAMED ENTITY RECOGNITION

DEFINITION AND PURPOSE
Named Entity Recognition is commonly abbreviated as NER.
NER is an information-extraction task in Natural Language Processing.
NER identifies spans of text that refer to meaningful real-world entities.
NER assigns a category or label to a recognized entity.
NER converts part of unstructured text into structured information.
An entity may be a person, organization, place, date, product, or event.
NER can also identify quantities, money values, percentages, and times.
NER helps a computer determine which words carry entity-level meaning.
NER is different from ordinary keyword extraction.
NER is also different from part-of-speech tagging.
Keyword extraction finds important terms without necessarily assigning entity types.
Part-of-speech tagging identifies grammatical categories such as noun and verb.
NER focuses on semantic entity categories.
NER is useful in search systems and information retrieval.
NER is useful in document analysis.
NER is useful in question-answering systems.
NER is useful in chatbots and conversational systems.
NER is useful in news and media analysis.
NER is useful in domain-specific information extraction.
NER can reduce the amount of unstructured text that downstream systems must process.

COMMON ENTITY TYPES
PERSON is a common NER label for names of people.
ORGANIZATION is a common NER label for companies and institutions.
LOCATION is commonly used for geographical places.
GPE can represent geopolitical entities such as countries and cities.
DATE identifies calendar dates or date expressions.
TIME identifies expressions referring to time.
MONEY identifies monetary values.
PERCENT identifies percentage expressions.
PRODUCT can identify named products.
EVENT can identify named events.
FACILITY can identify certain constructed locations.
WORK_OF_ART can identify titles of creative works in some datasets.
LAW can identify named laws in some annotation schemes.
LANGUAGE can identify language names in some datasets.
CARDINAL can identify numerical quantities in some tag sets.
ORDINAL can identify ordered numerical expressions.
QUANTITY can identify measurements and quantities.
NORP can identify nationalities, religious groups, or political groups in some datasets.
Entity labels depend on the dataset and annotation guidelines.
The same entity may receive different labels in different NER datasets.

EXAMPLES
In the sentence 'Ravi studies at Aditya University', Ravi can be labeled PERSON.
In the same sentence, Aditya University can be labeled ORGANIZATION.
In 'India is a country', India can be labeled GPE or LOCATION.
In 'The exam is on Monday', Monday can be labeled DATE.
In 'The meeting starts at 10 AM', 10 AM can be labeled TIME.
In 'The phone costs 50000 rupees', the amount can be labeled MONEY.
In 'The discount is 20 percent', 20 percent can be labeled PERCENT.
In 'Microsoft released a product', Microsoft can be labeled ORGANIZATION.
In 'Paris is in France', Paris and France are geographical entities.
In 'Dr. Rao visited Hyderabad', Dr. Rao can be a PERSON entity.
Entity boundaries are important in NER.
Multiword entities often require several tokens to be grouped together.
New York can be represented as one location entity.
Natural Language Processing can be treated as a phrase rather than a named entity depending on context.
Context helps distinguish ambiguous words.
Apple can refer to a company or a fruit.
The correct entity label for Apple depends on its context.
Washington can refer to a person, state, or place depending on context.
NER systems therefore need contextual information.

BIO TAGGING
BIO is a common token-level scheme for representing entity spans.
B means the beginning of an entity.
I means a token is inside an entity.
O means the token is outside a named entity.
A multiword entity usually begins with a B tag.
Following tokens of the same entity can receive I tags.
Tokens unrelated to entities receive O tags.
BIO tagging preserves entity boundaries.
BIO tagging can represent adjacent entities.
For 'New York', New can receive B-LOCATION and York can receive I-LOCATION.
For 'John Smith', John can receive B-PERSON and Smith can receive I-PERSON.
BIO tags are often written as B-PERSON, I-PERSON, and O.
BIO is also called IOB in some NLP literature.
Other tagging schemes include BILOU and BIOES.
Different schemes provide different ways to represent single-token entities.
Training data must follow a consistent tagging scheme.
Incorrect boundaries can reduce NER performance.
Entity spans are often evaluated using exact matching.
Token-level evaluation and entity-level evaluation can produce different results.

NER METHODS AND MODELS
Early NER systems often used manually written rules.
Gazetteers are lists of known entity names.
Gazetteers can help recognize organizations, locations, and people.
Rule-based NER can work well in restricted domains.
Rule-based systems can require substantial manual maintenance.
Statistical NER systems learn patterns from annotated training data.
Hidden Markov Models were used in earlier sequence-labeling systems.
Maximum Entropy models have also been used for NER.
Conditional Random Fields are a classic statistical sequence model for NER.
CRFs model relationships between neighboring output labels.
Neural networks are widely used for modern NER.
Recurrent neural networks can model sequential context.
BiLSTM models can process context in both directions.
Character representations can help handle spelling and morphology.
Transformer models provide contextual representations.
BERT can be fine-tuned for NER.
Modern transformer NER systems can use contextual token representations.
Pre-trained language models can reduce the amount of task-specific training required.
Domain-specific fine-tuning can improve recognition in specialized text.
NER quality depends on training data, labels, domain, and model design.

NER WORKFLOW
A typical NER workflow begins with raw text.
Text may be normalized before entity recognition.
Tokenization divides text into tokens.
The NER model processes token or subword representations.
The model predicts an entity label for each relevant token.
Predicted token labels are combined into entity spans.
Post-processing may normalize recognized entities.
Recognized entities can be stored in structured records.
Structured entities can be passed to search or question-answering modules.
NER can be combined with relation extraction.
Relation extraction can identify relationships between recognized entities.
Entity linking can connect a recognized mention to a knowledge-base entry.
Coreference resolution can connect different mentions of the same entity.
NER is often one stage in a larger information-extraction pipeline.

APPLICATIONS
Search engines can use NER to interpret named entities in queries.
Question-answering systems can use NER to identify the subject of a question.
NER can help locate a requested person in a knowledge base.
NER can help identify dates when answering historical questions.
NER can help distinguish organizations from locations.
News systems can extract people and organizations from articles.
Customer-support systems can extract product names from user messages.
Financial systems can recognize companies and monetary values.
Medical systems can recognize diseases and medicines when trained for that domain.
Legal NLP systems can identify laws, courts, organizations, and dates.
Social-media analysis can identify people, brands, places, and events.
NER can support document indexing.
NER can improve faceted search.
NER can support automatic metadata generation.
NER can assist knowledge-graph construction.

LIMITATIONS AND EVALUATION
NER systems can struggle with ambiguous names.
NER systems can struggle with rare entities.
NER systems can struggle with spelling variations.
NER systems can struggle with informal language.
Domain shift can reduce NER accuracy.
Training data can contain annotation inconsistencies.
Different datasets can use different entity definitions.
Precision measures how many predicted entities are correct.
Recall measures how many relevant entities are successfully found.
F1-score combines precision and recall.
Entity-level evaluation can require exact span and label matching.
Partial matches may be counted differently by different evaluation protocols.
High precision with low recall means many real entities are missed.
High recall with low precision means many predicted entities are incorrect.
NER errors can affect downstream question-answering systems.

NER IN QUESTION ANSWERING
A question-answering system can use NER during question analysis.
In 'Who founded Microsoft?', Microsoft can be identified as an organization.
In 'Where is the Eiffel Tower?', Eiffel Tower can be treated as a named landmark.
In 'When was the company founded?', the expected answer type may be a DATE.
In 'Who wrote Hamlet?', the expected answer type is a PERSON.
Expected answer types can help rank candidate answers.
NER can help filter documents containing the required entity.
NER can help match question entities with entities in knowledge-base documents.
NER can improve retrieval when a query contains proper names.
NER can be combined with TF-IDF or embeddings for document retrieval.
NER can be combined with question classification.
NER can help identify constraints such as location and date.
NER should not be treated as a complete question-answering solution.
NER recognizes entity mentions but does not by itself determine the final answer.
