Embeddings are numerical representations of words, phrases, sentences, documents, or other objects that allow computers to represent semantic and contextual information in the form of vectors.

In Natural Language Processing, embeddings are important because computers cannot directly understand human language as humans do. Text must be converted into numerical representations that machine-learning models can process.

Instead of representing a word only as a symbolic identifier, an embedding represents it as a vector of numbers.

For example:

"cat" → [0.21, -0.14, 0.67, 0.35, ...]

"dog" → [0.19, -0.11, 0.64, 0.39, ...]

The exact numbers are learned by an embedding model.

WHAT IS AN EMBEDDING?

An embedding is a dense numerical vector that represents an object in a continuous mathematical space.

In NLP, the object can be:

A word
A token
A sentence
A paragraph
A document
A question
A piece of text

The purpose of an embedding is to represent meaningful relationships between pieces of information.

Texts with similar meanings can have similar vector representations.

For example:

"How do computers understand language?"

and

"How can machines process human language?"

have different words, but their meanings are similar. A good semantic embedding model should therefore produce vectors that are relatively close to each other.

WHY EMBEDDINGS ARE IMPORTANT

Traditional computers process numerical data efficiently, but natural language contains meaning, context, ambiguity, and relationships between words.

Embeddings convert language into numerical representations that machine-learning systems can process.

Embeddings are useful for:

Semantic search
Question answering
Document retrieval
Text similarity
Recommendation systems
Classification
Clustering
Information retrieval
Retrieval-Augmented Generation
Chatbots
Duplicate detection
Document organization

For a question answering system, embeddings are especially useful because they allow the system to find information that is semantically related to a user's question.

VECTOR REPRESENTATION

An embedding is usually represented as a vector.

Example:

Text:

"Natural Language Processing"

could be represented conceptually as:

[0.12, -0.43, 0.71, 0.09, 0.55, ...]

The vector may contain hundreds or thousands of numerical dimensions depending on the embedding model.

Each dimension is a numerical value learned by the model.

The individual dimensions normally do not have simple human-readable meanings such as:

dimension 1 = language
dimension 2 = computer
dimension 3 = education

Instead, the information is distributed across many dimensions.

DIMENSIONS OF EMBEDDINGS

The number of values in an embedding vector is called its dimensionality.

For example:

[0.2, 0.5, -0.1]

has 3 dimensions.

Another embedding could contain hundreds or thousands of dimensions.

Example:

Embedding A:

[0.21, 0.42, -0.13, 0.71, ...]

Embedding B:

[0.18, 0.39, -0.11, 0.68, ...]

The dimensionality depends on the embedding model.

Higher dimensional representations can encode complex relationships, but they also require more storage and computational resources.
