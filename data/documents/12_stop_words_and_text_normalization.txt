TOP WORDS AND TEXT NORMALIZATION

1. Text Normalization
Text normalization converts text into a more consistent representation before NLP processing.

2. Common Operations
- Lowercasing
- Whitespace normalization
- Punctuation handling
- Unicode normalization
- Spelling normalization
- Stop-word processing
- Stemming
- Lemmatization

3. Purpose
Normalization can reduce unnecessary variation, improve matching, standardize input, and reduce feature space in traditional NLP.

4. Lowercasing
Natural Language Processing → natural language processing.
Case can carry information, so lowercasing should be task-dependent.

5. Whitespace Normalization
Repeated whitespace can be reduced.
"natural   language" → "natural language"

6. Punctuation
Punctuation may be removed, preserved, or standardized depending on the task.

7. Unicode Normalization
Different Unicode representations can be standardized.

8. Spelling Normalization
Spelling variants can sometimes be standardized.
colour ↔ color.
The policy depends on language and application.

9. Stop Words
Stop words are frequent words that may provide limited value for some traditional retrieval tasks.
Examples: the, a, an, is, of, to, in.

10. Stop-Word Removal
Selected words are removed.
"The cat is on the mat" → "cat mat"

11. Why Remove Stop Words
In some traditional systems, frequent function words increase index or feature size without adding much topical information.

12. Stop Words Can Matter
They can be important for negation, sentiment, syntax, QA, and phrase meaning.

13. Negation
Removing "not" can change meaning.
"I am happy" and "I am not happy" have opposite meanings.

14. Modern NLP
Transformer models generally do not require aggressive stop-word removal.
Function words can contribute to contextual meaning.

15. Search
Traditional search may remove stop words to reduce index size.
Modern search systems may preserve them depending on retrieval design.

16. Stop-Word Lists
A stop-word list is a predefined collection selected for a particular task or language.

17. Language Dependence
Stop-word lists are language-specific.

18. Domain Dependence
A word common in general language may be important in a specialized domain.
Stop-word decisions should consider the application.

19. Tokenization
A common flow is:
Raw Text → Normalization → Tokenization → NLP Processing.
The exact order can vary.

20. Information Retrieval
Normalization can improve matching between queries and documents.
"Computer Networks" and "computer networks" can match after case normalization.

21. Traditional NLP
Classical machine-learning pipelines often use normalization to reduce vocabulary variation.

22. Transformer Pipelines
Modern Transformers use model-specific tokenizers.
External preprocessing should not remove information expected by the model.

23. Text Cleaning
Cleaning may include removing unwanted HTML, fixing encoding, normalizing whitespace, removing artifacts, and standardizing text.

24. Special Characters
Special characters can be meaningful in code, mathematics, URLs, and technical documents.
They should not be removed blindly.

25. Numbers
Numbers can be important in QA and should not be removed when they contain facts.

26. Text Normalization in QA
Normalization can improve retrieval but must preserve names, numbers, negation, technical terms, and other evidence.

27. Text Normalization in RAG
Possible pipeline:
Documents → Light Cleaning → Chunking → Embeddings → Vector Search → Context → Transformer.

28. Embeddings
Embedding models often handle natural language variation without aggressive manual normalization.

29. Risks of Over-Normalization
It can remove useful context, change meaning, remove negation, destroy formatting, remove numbers or names, and harm retrieval.

30. Safe Normalization
Normalize unnecessary variation while preserving information relevant to the task.

31. Stop Words + Lemmatization
Tokenization → Stop-word Processing → Lemmatization.

32. Stop Words + Stemming
Tokenization → Stop-word Processing → Stemming → Indexing.

33. Source Preservation
For QA systems, preserve original text and source metadata.
A normalized representation can be stored separately.

34. Evaluation
Test whether normalization improves retrieval precision, retrieval recall, QA accuracy, search quality, or classification performance.

35. Task-Specific Design
There is no universally correct normalization pipeline.
Choices depend on language, model, domain, and task.

36. Final Summary
Text normalization standardizes input.
Stop-word removal can reduce feature size in traditional NLP but may remove important meaning.
Modern Transformers often need less manual preprocessing.
For QA and RAG, preserve facts, names, numbers, negation, and evidence needed for accurate retrieval and generation.
